# Known limitations

> **Update 2026-11-26 (QNX 8.0.6).** Current QNX 8.0.6 limits are summarized in `README.md` under *Scope, Measured Limits & Honest Disclosures*. The four policy-enforced (`secpol`) control-matrix runs and their logs: `EV3H` `45 ms` (`evidence/qnx_806/logs/ev3h_qnx_secpol.serial.clean.log:911`), `AS1` `48 ms` (`as1_qnx_secpol.serial.clean.log:911`), `AS2` `44 ms` (`as2_qnx_secpol.serial.clean.log:911`) pass the `50 ms` actuation-loop criterion; `EV3I` misses it at `97 ms` (`ev3i_qnx_secpol.serial.clean.log:930`, `1` of `135` trials over, host load peak `4.58` in `ev3i_host_load.log`). The `nopol` runs `A1` / `A2` (`45` / `47 ms`, `a1_nopol_qnx_nopol.serial.clean.log:910`, `a2_nopol_qnx_nopol.serial.clean.log:910`) are separate. The gate-death ranges `5–10 ms` (detection), `6–10 ms` (onset-to-brake) and `304–306 ms` (onset-to-safe) are the four `J24A` boots `A1`, `A2`, `AS1`, `AS2` (line `911` / `912` of each log). Other limits: pre-registered v1 budget premise `FAIL` as stale tails stayed `46.1–243.0 ms <= 500 ms`; test-only `--resv-time-div 5` on device-path reservation runs; QEMU TCG relative timings only. Fairer baselines: in earlier host runs a car that always yields or yields at random also had `0.00%` take-overs, the same as our rule, and the waiting car given our map had the same take-over rate as without it (`evidence/fair_baseline_20261117/NOTES.md`); the headline compares our rule with a car that never yields on its own. The historical engineering record below documents earlier hardening rounds (`2026-11-04`–`2026-11-11`).

This is a hackathon prototype. In a first review round we had it attacked by five independent reviewers (judge view, code defects, protocol logic, claims audit, release readiness) and kept the findings; a second review round added the checks named in the next heading. This file separates what is fixed from what is open.

## Fixed after the review (covered by `make -C qnx_target test`, 114 checks; a second review round added the quota, expired-slot reuse and summary-truncation checks)
- Implausible frames (zero or huge size, heading or position outside the map, line endpoints outside the map) are rejected.
- For one pseudo-ID only a newer frame replaces the stored one (16-bit serial arithmetic on `t_ms`), so old and new frames in any order give the same result.
- `ttl_ms` is capped at 300 ms and 0 is refused; expired but not yet swept frames are ignored by the decision.
- A pseudo-ID is bound to the process (pid, uid) that first wrote it, for as long as its frame is alive; the device is `0660`; one process may hold at most 4 IDs, so it cannot fill the 16-slot cache. A frame that has expired counts as a free slot, so a restarted sender can reuse its ID.
- The decision is returned in one piece, so two readers cannot get a mixed report. Read protocol: use a buffer of at least `sizeof(v2v_arbitration_report_t)` (1144 bytes: the decision plus up to 6 relayed votes); the first `read()` after `open()` returns the decision, a second `read()` on the same descriptor returns end-of-file until you `lseek(fd, 0, SEEK_SET)` or reopen.
- A decision list that does not fit the 480-byte summary is cut with `...`.
- The server refuses to run if dropping privileges or `procmgr_ability` fails.

## Open: protocol
- **The ID binding does not outlive the frame for unsigned frames.** With signatures required (the default on QNX) a frame is bound to its certificate and sequence number; re-sending the same certificate keeps the sequence number and an older certificate is refused (test T36). With `--allow-unsigned`, after a frame expires (300 ms) another local process allowed to write the device can take the ID.
- **Frame signing is a prototype of the real thing.** Frames are signed (ECDSA P-256) under short-lived certificates, so changed, replayed and unsigned frames and foreign or expired certificates are refused. Still missing: the fleet CA is simulated on the same machine and its key sits in `/dev/shmem`; validity uses the receiver's monotonic clock, not GNSS/UTC; there is no revocation list; pseudo-IDs are not rotated with the certificates; and a vehicle that holds a valid certificate can still lie about its position.
- **The vote step agrees on the plan, not on its execution.** A car moves only when all members' votes match, but it cannot know that the others also saw all votes (the classic two-generals limit): in the loss simulation some cars move while others hold (12 % of first rounds at 30 % loss). In this scene that is safe because a holding car stands still, but it is not a general proof. There is still no "I have started / finished moving" message: if the car chosen to give way stops half-way, nothing notices.
- **Members come from the hand-shake.** A vehicle that has left but whose certificate is still valid keeps everyone holding for 3 s (then it is silent and no longer a member); its last footprint stays in the plan as a static obstacle until its certificate ends (60 s in the demo), so a car that drove off can still block a plan through its old position for that long; there is no leave message.
- **On QNX the votes travel through the device, the frames do not.** `/dev/v2v_ttl` relays the votes between the three car processes (each checks the vote signatures itself, so the relay cannot forge one, but it could drop or delay them). Each car process takes the other cars' frames, certificates and member list from the client's memory, inherited through `fork()`, and does not verify the frames' signatures itself; only the device verifies them when they are written. Every write reaches the device through the gateway, so the device logs the gateway's pid for all of them. Loss, duplication, replay and reordering are tested in an in-process radio model, not over a real link.
- **One deadlock at a time.** With two independent standoffs only one yielder is chosen.
- **Silent vehicles.** A car that does not broadcast is not in the wait graph (a human driver, for example).
- **Frame loss makes receivers compute different plans.** The vote step turns that into "everyone holds" (by rule; checked in 12,000 simulated trials at 0–50 % loss), but high loss means long waits: at 50 % loss only 41 % of trials finish within 5 rounds.
- **The plan's cost is simple.** Fewest moves first, then the distance of backing and side steps; it does not model speed, time, steering or comfort. A side step is only offered to two-ended vehicles (at least 30 cm) and is made as a reverse crab at a fixed ratio (145 cm back per 90 cm across, about 32°); the planner checks its whole swept area and end pose with a 50 cm margin (test T59), but not the wheel-angle change or the speed. A back-off is as long as the car's room allows (room minus a 70 cm end margin, at most 700 cm, at least 450 cm); a car that has backed keeps its new place as an obstacle for the cars that back after it until it drives on (planner version 6; state 5 of the search). **No test pins that rule alone**: T11 and the reference plan (B's room is 610 cm because C stands at its new place) exercise it as a whole, and a version of the code that forgets the rule in the sweep was not caught by the host tests (tried 2026-11-10: the reference plan reaches the same order by another path); a scene that does separate the two was not found. The search covers up to 6 vehicles; more fall back to the simple rule (most free space gives way). It also judges free rear distance and free bay width in centimetres, and uses fixed scenario constants (450 to 700 cm back-off, 50 cm margin, 450 cm bay reach, 600 cm look-ahead).
- **Look-ahead is short and straight.** 600 cm is well under one second at road speeds, and the corridor ignores curves.
- **Cache admission when full** (more than 16 distinct IDs) depends on arrival order.

- **Self-routing moves the car's outline without turning.** A car parked at an angle keeps that angle along its route; the route is a shortest path on a 20 cm grid, not a drivable trajectory. In the reference scene, with the 50 cm margin, no car finds a route of its own (car B, turned in at -165° and about 3.1 m across at that angle, finds none between the human-driven car and the parked cars on the far side, even at 20 cm), so only the multi-car plan moves them (scenario S1). The entry check treats every object that sends nothing in the oncoming half as possibly moving toward the car (one frame cannot tell parked from moving); with consecutive frames a real system could tell.
- **Four lines per frame limit what the joint plan can know.** With more objects than the remaining cars can carry, some edges are not sent (scenario family F3: 7 of 100 scenes end with a car stopped short of a pedestrian it only partly knows). Motion of objects that send nothing comes from the receivers' tracking between pictures, which the simulation gives them directly.
- **The position-jump check only delays a bad position.** A refused jump is accepted once the old frame has expired (300 ms); taking the faulty car out is the job of its own watchdog board, which catches jumps at once and drifts late (see below).
- **The watchdog is tuned on its own noise model.** The board now sees only the noisy localiser and odometry (no true error), but the noise levels, drift rates and thresholds are model choices. A drift is caught late (mean 72 cm, worst 155 cm of error), so a wrong picture over 1 m was still possible in 28 ticks against 3,366 without the board (evidence/watchdog_sim_20261105.log); 1 of 47 drifts of 5-10 cm/s was not caught in its run. 0 false alarms in 33 fault-free car-hours bounds the false-alarm rate only loosely. One fault in the batch was still recovering when its run ended (1317 rejoined + 18 escalated = 1335 of 1336 detected).
- **The watchdog board and the 4G link are simulated**, including the fleet centre, the remote restart (fails 1 in 5 in the model) and the latencies. Remote restart is meant only for a stopped car.

## Open: simulation and evidence
- Sensing, line extraction and map matching are simulated by 2D ray casting of one static scene rebuilt from a social-media clip; in round 2 each car writes from its own process, but all three are forked by one client on one machine. The clip's street is not identified. Measured on three frames: kerb to car B's nose on the 2 s frame (SAM masks, scale from a tyre); car A about 116 cm from the kerb on the 6 s frame, scaled by its own 1.9 m width (the scene uses 110 cm; the earlier gutter-grid read is retired); along-street positions on the 25 s frame (scale from car B's wheelbase). The other positions are estimates.
- The human-driven car behind car A sends no frame. The others see it (it is in their outline lines), but the plan cannot ask it to move.
- No radio link and no vehicle dynamics or control (the motion in the animations is scripted for illustration); packet loss only in the in-process radio model (independent or Gilbert-Elliott burst loss per copy, certificates included; no delay distribution).
- Timings come from QEMU software emulation. The timing run (`evidence/qnx_run_20261109_final5.log`, the build before the 50 cm change; the planner is the only timing path that changed, one decision now takes 15.3 ms) shows 51.9 ms round trip (with three signature checks) and 32.9 ms server compute (one run; earlier runs of other builds showed 47.8 and 29.8 ms, 52.3 and 33.0 ms, 44.4 and 27.0 ms, 47.8 and 29.0 ms, 41.2 and 22.3 ms, 44.6 and 22.3 ms, 38.7 and 21.3 ms and 28.0 and 19.9 ms: the emulated CPU varies with the load on the host). The decision cache (recompute only when the frame set changes) saves the cars' repeated reads, not this round trip. The 0.41–20.9 ms figures are the delay between a frame expiring and the next 20 ms sweep evicting it, which lies between 0 and 20 ms by design (the 20.9 ms value is a sweep that ran a little late under emulation), so they say nothing about timer accuracy. The sweep interval itself was read from one kernel trace (six intervals printed); a long run with percentiles is still to do.
- The jam replay in the animation (the first 4 s, shown 3x faster) and the gaps the standoff starts with are the clip's own, not a safety margin: car B to car C 20.9-25.2 cm and car A to car B 34.1 cm. `node visualizer/legal_gap_report.mjs` splits the motion into that replay and the planned manoeuvre: in the planned manoeuvre no two vehicles that move come closer than 50 cm, and no moving vehicle comes closer to a kerb than 50 cm, unless the gap was already smaller at the start and does not shrink (B to C, A to B and C to the far kerb at the start; car C eases 9 cm away from the kerb while it backs so that its tail swing at the pull-out does not shrink its kerb gap). The smallest gaps while passing are A to B 61.8 cm, C to the parked SUV 62.8 cm, B to the parked SUV 61 cm, B to the human-driven car 73.4 cm; `check_overlaps.mjs` still checks bodies at 20 cm and kerbs at 15 cm.
- **Road-law articles saved but not compared, or in conflict (review 2026-11-11; the review note is internal and not in the public tree).** Art. 101 (3) and penalty act art. 45 (2): an emergency vehicle needs an immediate move to the right, while the cars here wait for agreement and move toward the kerb. Art. 112 and penalty act art. 59: a broken-down vehicle must be moved if possible and a warning sign placed 5-30 m behind it; the watchdog board and family F5 only keep the car stopped. Art. 95, 97 (2), 100 (2) (3) (slopes, mountain roads, priority) are not modelled. This is a comparison with the text, not a legal opinion.
- **The emergency-vehicle animation was withdrawn (2026-11-09).** Its manoeuvre (car B backing about 7 m into the space car C leaves, to open a corridor for a fire engine) came from the 20 cm plan. With 50 cm kept, car B has already backed 450 cm along its own heading (a straight line, 1.2 m toward the far side), and a reverse S-curve with a 5.2 m radius from there into the bay needs about 3.4 m of travel along the street for the last 1.05 m (a geometric estimate with the animation's own path code, not a simulation), which puts the car's rear about 2.3 m beyond where car C stands and 1 m beyond the 9.3 m C has free once its end margin is kept. A narrower vehicle (210 cm, 240 cm over the mirrors) would fit the 345 cm corridor of the old scene, but car B cannot get out of that corridor. The pull-over rule stays in the protocol and in `--session-sim` (it does not compute the corridor); no animation or timing claim is made for it any more.
- **A flood of wrong chain values under one ID slows that car's recovery for up to a second.** The gateway spends at most 1000 SHA-256 per ID per second and, after 4 wrong values in a second, checks only one step for that ID until the second ends (test T60). A real frame of the flooded car that is more than one step ahead of the gateway's last value is refused in that second (errno 50). The QNX demo (`run_client()`) shows this: after the 60 forged frames under B's ID, car B's real frame is refused, the client prints that, waits 1.1 s and sends the next frame, which is accepted. The trade-off is intended: it keeps the gateway's hashing cost bounded; a lost second of one car's frames is the price.
- **A signed member's implausible frame costs a full ECDSA check before it is refused (measured, 2026-11-11).** On QNX under emulation the median is 336 us, the same as an accepted frame; header, CRC and plausibility inside `cache_upsert_core` run after the signature check. Moving them before it brings that class to about 9 us in a trial build (macro `V2V_EARLY_FRAME_CHECK`, off in the evidence build because the Rust port does not do the same reorder yet and a car that sends only implausible frames would stop counting as alive). Also measured: a vote with a forged signature runs its ECDSA check inside the state lock when it arrives through `io_write` (about 320 us per forged vote while the lock is held). Numbers are emulator time (the timing-trial logs are in the evaluation archive, not in the public tree).
- **One certified member can keep everyone from committing, and nothing removes it.** Reproduced with `scripts/rogue_member.c` (no radio, the device's own vote and agreement code, 100 rounds = 30 s): a member that keeps sending valid frames but votes another plan hash, or sends no vote at all, leaves both honest cars at 0 commits in 100 rounds (a vote for a future round is refused with -ERANGE, so it is the same as no vote) (the honest control commits in all 100). Since 2026-11-09 the first vote of a car in a round is the one that counts and a second, different vote in the same round is refused as noise (T29), so a car that merely changes its vote no longer holds everyone (that variant now commits in 100 of 100); a car whose FIRST vote is the bad one still does, and nothing removes it. The cars hold and after 1.5 s without a ruling ask for help, so the stall is not silent, but it is a stall that one compromised or faulty certified car can cause for as long as it keeps its certificate. Removing such a member (the design rule "an equivocation proof turns the member into a static obstacle and the others re-form the member set", `DESIGN_LOCKED_SESSION_20261104.md`) is not built, and in this scene it would not free the street anyway if the rogue car is car C, the car that has to back off. Since 2026-11-09 a vote that has my frame digest and frame count but another plan hash names its voter as faulty or lying (`v2v_agree_dissenters`, test T65; a car that merely missed a frame votes another digest and is not accused; the silent members drawn as obstacles are part of the digest for that reason): the QNX car process prints the name, and no decision changes. With three cars nobody can be outvoted (classical Byzantine agreement needs n >= 3f + 1), so the cars still hold. The in-process radio simulation (`--agree-sim`, `--session-sim`) has no single relay, so there two different votes of one car still make the receivers hold.
- **Clocks are not shared between cars in this prototype.** The trust clock is each machine's own `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`. A certificate's validity and a frame's generation time are seconds-since-boot values that the receiver compares with its own clock; two cars booted at different times would differ by far more than the 300 ms frame life and the 200 ms allowed lead, and would refuse each other's frames; the vote rounds (16 per second) need agreement to about 60 ms. Measured (test T66): a join or frame is accepted only when the receiver's clock is within [-200 ms, +300 ms) of the sender's, otherwise `-ETIME`. QNX documents that `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` is never affected by `ClockAdjust()` or time of day changes, so a PTP or GNSS discipline cannot reach the trust clock as it is built; see `docs/TIME_SYNC_DESIGN_20261109.md`. The QNX demo runs all cars on one machine, where this holds by construction. A real system needs a shared time source (UTC from GNSS, gPTP) and a stated offset budget; production note 1 below speaks of drift only, which understates this.
- **The hash chain does not stop an attacker who can hear the radio.** Chain values are broadcast. An attacker in range can send a frame with a heard chain value and a bad signature before the real car's frame; the device checks each chain value at most twice and the gateway drops a third try (since 2026-11-09 the refusal is charged to the chain value; before, four refusals blocked the victim's ID for 1 s and test T49 showed 37 of 40 frames dropped). One fake per value now costs the victim nothing (40 extra signature checks at the device in 2 s); two fakes per value still cost it every frame and a re-join (the device checks only 2 x 9 values, then the chain is out of the 9-step window), at a price of two attacker frames per victim frame. It needs no certificate and little power. "Forged frames never reach the device" holds for an attacker who does not hear the car's broadcasts, and for votes since they carry a chain value too (T62). Charging the penalty to the physical source instead of the pseudo-ID (production note 3) is not built.
- **The agreement numbers are measured without the 3 s silent-member rule.** The agreement and session simulations (12,000 and more trials, no car ever on a different plan) model loss, duplication, corruption and replay, but not the device's rule that drops a member that has been silent for 3 s out of the member set (tests T50, T56). A dropped member whose car is in fact still moving is treated as a static obstacle at its last position, which is wrong for a moving car; the signed "step started/done" messages that would tell the others are simulated only. The invariant is therefore shown for the model without that rule. An exhaustive check of the vote for three cars (`scripts/model_check_agree.py`, log `evidence/model_check_agree_20261109.log`) shows: with one device deciding the member set, agreement and \"no moving obstacle\" hold in all 448 view combinations; without a relay they hold only with the rule that a car never commits alone (262,144 combinations; the in-process simulation has that rule); and turning a member that has voted into a static obstacle after it falls silent breaks \"no moving obstacle\" for every policy except freezing the voted members. Since 2026-11-09 a member that has voted is not dropped as an obstacle for 30 s after its last frame: the arbitration computes no plan and the cars hold and ask for help (test T64). The model is an abstraction of the rule, not of the C code.
- **No hazard analysis, no FTTI, no safety integrity allocation.** The time constants (20 ms sweep, 300 ms frame life, 1.5 s no-ruling, 3 s silent member) were chosen from the simulations, not derived from a fault-tolerant time interval; the 50 cm planning margin (the legal half metre, not derived from sensor error) leaves only 11.8 cm over the smallest passing gap of the reference animation (61.8 cm), less than the uncertainty of the measured scene (a few tens of cm) and of real vehicle positioning (see the internal review note of 2026-11-11, not in the public tree). The watchdog simulation's 72 cm (mean) and 155 cm (worst) are how far off a FAULTY localiser already was when the watchdog caught its drift, not the nominal position error; the margin was not derived from either number. Any safety-related wording in the production notes is a design intention, not an analysis.
- **Planning margins.** Side steps, routes, backing and the end poses keep 50 cm (`LEGAL_GAP_CM`); only the side-step margin changes the plan in the reference scene. The passing corridor keeps none; vehicle sizes are rounded to 10 cm and mirrors are not in the footprint. The scene's own measurement uncertainty (a few tens of cm) is as large as the margin, so 50 cm in the plan is not 50 cm on a road. Backing looks every 10 cm across the body and 50 cm beyond each side, and needs 70 cm more than the move (50 cm margin plus one 20 cm grid cell) (test T63, X18).
- **The "secure page" is ordinary RAM.** The gateway's chain table lives in a page from the typed-memory object `/sysram` (`posix_typed_mem_open`), is `mlock()`ed, and is zeroised and read back on an orderly stop. `/sysram` is the system RAM, not an isolated region; `RLIMIT_CORE` is set to 0 and read back, but whether `dumper` honours it was not tested with a real crash, and the page's content after `SIGKILL` was not examined. No test shows that another uid cannot map it.
- **The plan was compared with a road law; the comparison is partial.** Read from the official database (page and text in `evidence/law/`, excerpts `LAW_EXCERPTS_20261109.md`): Taiwan Road Traffic Safety Rules art. 100 (5) (meeting vehicles keep at least half a metre between them); art. 110 (backing: show the reverse light, back carefully and slowly, watch other vehicles and pedestrians, not on a section with a narrow-road, bend, slope, bridge, roundabout, tunnel or one-way sign; the article's proviso "except where reversing is necessary to give way, park, or start moving" exempts backing that is needed to give way, stop or start, and the planned backing in this scene is a give-way back-off, so it may fall under that wording (whether a 700 cm back-off is 'necessary' is not shown by the text): this is our reading of the text (see `evidence/law/`), not a legal opinion); Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act art. 46 and 50 are the penalty side of the same rules. The plan and the animation now keep 50 cm between vehicles that move (see above); the standoff's own start (20-34 cm) is inherited from the clip. The street is not known to be free of a narrow-road sign, so car B's backing is not shown to be lawful there (art. 110). Nothing here is a statement that the manoeuvre is lawful on any road. Before this change the planner kept 20 cm; with 50 cm it found another plan (first hash 0x7ABBE703 with every back-off 450 cm, then 0x547AA342, now 0x4F390A9A).
- **QNX runs are not always green.** Of the 15 QNX runs of the patched build on 2026-11-09, 13 passed all 9 programs and 2 failed (`bench_b` once, `test_qnx_secmem` once, same source). The `bench_b` failure was reproduced: a 300 ms frame life and an emulator that stalls for longer (a red-team run froze QEMU for 350 ms every second or so) make the bench fail exactly like that run; the first fix (warm-up, retry on an empty decision) was not enough and the bench now also retries a write refused with `ETIME`. The `test_qnx_secmem` failure was not reproduced and its cause is unknown. The unmodified main line failed its own QNX client check (`client rc=1`) before the port. The full table with source revisions and the stall evidence is in the README ("QNX runs of the port"). Seven passes in a row do not prove that no other intermittent failure is left.
- Hardware acceleration (HVF) did not boot QNX on our arm64 macOS host; only software emulation (TCG) was used. The `x86_64` build compiles but has not been run.
- The kernel trace covers 3 s of a second client run. Pulse intervals are converted with an assumed 62.5 MHz timer (QEMU virt default), not read from the target, and only the first pulses (five intervals) are printed.
- The priority bench runs 60 round trips per setting on emulated CPUs with two simple busy-loop hogs, one run. In the current run, at priority 30 inheritance gives a median of 30.8 ms (p95 36.2) against 63.6 ms (p95 78.7) on the fixed-priority control; the client's own priority alone gains about 6 ms over priority 10, so here inheritance gives most of the gain. Earlier runs split the gain differently. It is not a worst-case bound on real hardware.
- The side step uses all the free room minus the 50 cm margin, not the smallest step that would clear the nose (in the reference scene no step fits; tests T13, T44, T59 use scenes where one does).

- **Simulator comment on the ablation mask bit order (B4M2 tree).** The header comment near qnx_target/src/v2v_spec2_sim.inc.c:4882 in the frozen B4M2 copy (qnx_api_b4m2, kept outside this repository) describes the mask bit order the opposite way from the code, which sets bits at :4941 and prints the bytes at :4950 (bytes in order, trial t = bit t%8 of byte t//8). The frozen tree is not edited; the analysis script seed_cluster_ci.py follows the code. The comment was to be corrected by task J24 (the J24 patch has since been run on QNX; see the README).

## Not claimed
Safety certification, real-time guarantees on automotive hardware, resistance to a malicious vehicle, or any result with real sensors.
- **QNX features not used yet (state of the 2026-11-09 boot demo; the `J24B` build adds `slm` supervision that restarts a killed planner, see the README).** No High Availability Manager (the image has none: `test_qnx_probe` checked ham, libham and aps; the boot demo does not restart a crashed server; `v2v-supervisor`, a plain respawn loop and not HAM, is used only by `test_qnx_supervisor`), and the car processes in the demo share one uid (each car writes under its own certificate, but not under its own uid). `ionotify()` reports input as an edge only, so `poll()`/`select()` for input are not supported; the car processes wait on it for the other votes, and arming it sometimes returns `EBUSY` when they arm at the same moment (retried after 2 ms; cause not investigated). The cycle timings come from QEMU software emulation and say nothing about real hardware.
- **Security hardening of round 5 (2026-11-04) and what it leaves open.** Fixed after a red-team review with working attacks: stale frames replayed later (frames now carry a signed 32-bit generation time and are refused when older than their TTL), the 16-bit sequence wrap, frames and votes sharing one counter, certified outsiders pushing the relay round, setting a global conflict flag or filling the 6 relayed votes (one slot per car, votes only from cars with a live frame), replayed certificates filling the cache, a car that missed a hand-shake committing on a smaller plan (member set in the voted digest, certificates re-sent every round, unknown cars make it hold, a car that hears nobody does not commit), and a plan hash that left out distances (now SHA-256 over every step). Still open: in the QNX demo the three car processes are forks of one client that holds all three private keys and the CA key; frames there never cross a radio; anyone with the CA key can issue certificates for any number of IDs (Sybil); a member that lies consistently is invisible to the vote; a member can vote another plan hash to make everyone hold (a second, different vote in the same round is ignored); a reader can make the device re-run the decision only after the set of live frames has changed (round 9 decision cache; otherwise the cached decision is returned). Round 8 (2026-11-05): replaying valid certificates of absent cars about 8 times a second kept every new car out (0 of 17 hand-shakes) and kept the others holding; a certificate is now admitted only in one join message with a fresh frame signed by its key (cheap checks first, then the CA and frame signatures), so a certificate alone takes no slot and makes nobody hold, and a member's certificate is never pushed out (tests T39, T40, T45, T48). The gateway now keeps 200 messages per second for members, separate from the 200 shared by everything else (test T49). Still open after round 8: a join captured within its frame's 300 ms TTL can be replayed once; forged frames under one member's pseudo-ID can crowd out that member at the edge, because the gateway does not check signatures; a flood on the shared budget can delay a new car's join; each forged join or frame under a held certificate still costs the device one signature check under its lock. Round 8, third review (2026-11-05): three more attacks were reproduced on the round-8 code and fixed. (1) A valid join captured elsewhere and replayed within its 300 ms TTL was admitted as a member that never votes, and membership never shrank, so the others could not commit until its certificate ended (60 s in the test), and 16 such joins filled every slot; now a member with no validly signed frame for 3 s (2 decision windows) drops out of the member list and gives its slot to a car that proves its key, and the same join bytes are refused while held (`EALREADY`) and later (`ETIME`) (test T50: commit after 3.3 s, a real car admitted after 3.3 s). Round 9 (2026-11-05): the silent member's last accepted footprint is kept in the planner as a static obstacle (test T56). Challenge-response for joins is not built. (2) The gateway charged refusals to the pseudo-ID and moved a member with more than 20 refusals to the shared budget, so frames forged under member B's ID crowded B out; now budgets are kept per pseudo-ID and sender (the writing process in the demo, the radio source address on a real link), refusals are charged to the sender, and a sender with 4 bad signatures in a second is cut off for 1 s (round 8, test T49 of that build: a sender spoofing B's own address still cut B off, 40 of 40). Round 9 (2026-11-05): signed frames now carry the next value of the sender's 1024-value SHA-256 hash chain (8 bytes; frame 130 -> 138 bytes, join 275 -> 283), whose anchor is in the ECDSA-signed join; the gateway checks one SHA-256 per frame, allows 8 lost frames, drops a frame with a wrong value and charges it to the shared budget, never to the car whose ID it names (test T49: floods under other IDs and forgeries under B's ID from any sender, B's spoofed address included, reach the device 0 times and cost A, B and C nothing; tests T53-T55: 8 lost frames recover, 9 need a re-join, rollover at 64 values left, an old value is refused, one SHA-256 per frame). Residual: a sender that sees B's frame before the gateway does can race it with the same chain value and a bad signature; since 2026-11-09 a refusal is charged to the chain value (at most 2 device checks per value; test T49 prints it: one fake per value costs B nothing, two fakes per value cost B 40 of 40), which is still jamming-class. Round 9, finding R9-B1 (fixed 2026-11-08): the gateway ran up to 9 SHA-256 per junk frame before any budget; now an ID without a chain costs no hash, an ID with 4 wrong values in a second gets 1 hash per frame, and no ID gets more than 1,000 hashes a second (test T60: 5,000 junk frames per second under B cost 2,080 SHA-256 in 2 s, A and C lose nothing, B loses 36 of 40; at 400 per second nobody loses a frame); a car that lost frames while its ID is flooded recovers in the next second or with a join. Votes, joins and certificates carry no chain value, so their refusals are still charged to the sender's address, and a sender that spoofs B's address can get B's votes dropped for 1 s after 4 forged votes (the others hold). (3) Wrong-key joins with a CA-valid certificate each cost one frame-signature check under the device's lock; now one sender buys at most 4 per second (test T51: 14 checks in 2 s instead of one per join), and on QNX the signature checks of joins and frames run outside the state lock and are committed only after every check is repeated under it (test T52). A per-certificate backoff was not used: certificates are public, so anyone could trigger it for a real car's certificate and keep that car out. Still open: a sender with a new address for every message still buys one signature check per forged join or frame (within the shared 200 per second, outside the lock) and can delay a new car's join; votes are checked under the lock; the simulations keep every member for the whole trial. The plan hash is computed over a little-endian int32 encoding, so other makers would have to use exactly that canonical form.
- **The edge gateway is a process, not a board.** On QNX it is a separate process with its own uid in front of the device; on a car it would be a separate microcontroller or small computer on the radio side, so that a parser bug or a flood can take down the gateway but not the planner. Its rate limits (200 messages per second for members, 200 for everything else, 50 per ID charged after the device accepts) are design values, and it does not check signatures (the device does).
- **The locked session is a simulation (host and QNX boot), not vehicle code.** `--session-sim` models loss, timing, the pause lock, the emergency pull-over rule (without a corridor computation) and link loss for the one reference scene with scripted motion; it runs in-process (also at QNX boot, see evidence/qnx_run_20261109_final5.log), does not sign messages, including its "step done" (the signatures are tested separately; a signed "step done" is not built), and does not compute the emergency corridor's width. The emergency-vehicle animation that used to check it (3.45 m corridor, 27.5 cm each side of a fire engine's mirrors, from the earlier 20 cm planner) was withdrawn on 2026-11-09 (see above); these numbers are no longer claimed, and the pull-over rule has not been checked against art. 101 (3) of the Taiwan rules (move to the right at once). In 80 of 10,000 trials no car saw the person who walked through, and in all 80 the cars moved while the person was there (1,108 rounds of motion of 300 ms; evidence/session_sim_20261106.log); no protocol can stop for what nobody sees, and the model draws each car's detection independently, while a real occlusion would hide the person from several cars at once. The control centre's answer rates (70 / 15 / 15 %) are assumptions.

## Production notes (design intent, not reviewed by any safety authority)

These notes record ideas for a production system. They were written by the project team, are not implemented or tested in this prototype, and have not been reviewed by any safety, QNX or cybersecurity authority. Standards and product names are design intent, not certification or compliance claims.

1. **Distributed Clock Drift in GPS-Denied Environments (Tunnels / Underground Garages)**
   - *Limitation*: The prototype relies on monotonic clock timestamps for TTL validation (`CLOCK_MONOTONIC`). On real distributed hardware, independent quartz crystals exhibit temperature drift (typically 20–50 ppm), which can accumulate a 200 ms offset over ~33 minutes in GPS-denied environments, causing valid frames to be discarded with `-ETIME`.
   - *Production Mitigation*: Deploy a hybrid time-trust model combining GNSS 1PPS synchronization where available, IEEE 802.1AS (gPTP) over the vehicle Ethernet backbone, and C-V2X relative timestamping (`delta_t` relative to frame arrival time at the physical radio PHY layer), eliminating absolute drift dependencies.

2. **Local Collision Avoidance Keeps the Last Word**
   - *Limitation*: The protocol computes cooperative maneuvers over the air; a pure V2V agreement must never override a vehicle's own local collision avoidance. This prototype has no onboard perception or braking stack to arbitrate with, so local sensor veto remains a design intent only, not implemented or tested here.

3. **Gateway Penalty Decoupling Against Sniff-and-Race Attacks**
   - *Limitation*: A malicious adversary sniffing a broadcast frame can race the gateway with the same valid hash-chain token and a corrupted signature, inducing 4 verification failures and causing the gateway to block the victim vehicle's Pseudo-ID for 1 second.
   - *Production Mitigation*: Decouple rate-limiting and penalty counters from business Pseudo-IDs. Penalties must target the physical radio source (RF PHY signal strength, MAC layer link index, or antenna arrival angle), preventing an external attacker from weaponizing bad signatures against another vehicle.

4. **SOTIF Perception Gap & Collective Perception Service (CPS)**
   - *Limitation*: In 80 of 10,000 simulated trials none of the three cars saw the person (each car's detection is sampled independently in this model; real occlusion would be correlated between cars), so cars could move while the person was in the area.
   - *Production Mitigation*: While local sensing cannot avoid what is physically occluded, Ephemeral-V2V serves as the foundation for ETSI Collective Perception Messages (CPM). Integrating Roadside Units (RSU) at blind intersections provides overhead line-of-sight broadcasts to eliminate pedestrian blind spots prior to agreement.

5. **Anti-Forensic Memory Sanitization & Hardware Root of Trust (HSM)**
   - *Limitation*: Compilers with aggressive optimization (`-O2/-O3`) may eliminate standard `memset()` operations via Dead Store Elimination (DSE). Additionally, CA keys and pre-allocated hash chains in `/dev/shmem` are vulnerable to physical memory dumps.
   - *Production Mitigation*: Replace generic `memset()` with `OPENSSL_cleanse()` and C11 `memset_s()`. Store root CA certificates and ECDSA private keys in hardware-backed Secure Hardware Extensions (SHE) or Hardware Security Modules (HSM). Generate hash-chain elements dynamically on-demand from a single secure seed rather than exposing the entire chain in RAM.

6. **Microkernel Concurrency & ionotify Lock Discipline**
   - *Limitation*: Arming `ionotify` at the same moment from several processes sometimes returns `EBUSY` (see above: the cause has not been investigated; a lock-discipline problem is one possible cause, not verified).
   - *Production Mitigation*: Enforce strict QNX Resource Manager attribute lock discipline (`iofunc_attr_lock(&ocb->attr)`) across `io_notify`, `io_write` trigger, and `io_close_ocb`, eliminating race conditions on notification linked lists. Replace the monolithic `g_state_lock` with separate lightweight spinlocks for cache lookups and read-write locks for the trajectory arbiter.
